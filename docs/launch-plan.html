Go-to-market & launch plan

Turn a bill scanner into a bill defender.

BillGuard already reads a medical bill and explains the codes. The business is in what happens next: catching the error, winning the appeal, and telling people they got their money back.

Verdict: real problem, unfinished wedge Model: freemium → outcomes Path: bootstrap to traction, then raise

This document is the primary plan: publish the app, get it live, and grow it without outside money. The fundraising case lives in a separate dossier, and the discovery/acquisition tactics in a third. Read this one first — it defines the product and the sequence everything else hangs on.

The honest read: the market pain is enormous and durable, but a well-organized filing cabinet for bills is a feature, not a company. People don't pay a subscription to store medical bills — they pay to stop overpaying and to make the paperwork end. Every recommendation below pushes BillGuard from "understands your bill" toward "gets you a specific dollar outcome," because that is the only version investors, the App Store, and word-of-mouth reward.

01

What BillGuard is today

Grounding the plan in the actual product, not the ambition.

BillGuard is a SwiftUI app (iOS today, macOS + a browser extension in progress) that scans a paper or PDF medical bill, runs on-device OCR to pull out the provider, patient, insurance and procedure fields, and looks up CPT / HCPCS / ICD-10 codes against a bundled database with plain-English explanations and typical price ranges. It organizes bills, claims, insurance policies and payments per patient and syncs through iCloud/CloudKit. The macOS build adds file/PDF import (no camera) and a portal-scraping extension.

Strength

On-device, private

OCR and data stay on the user's devices via CloudKit. In a category defined by distrust, "your bills never hit our servers" is a real selling line.

Strength

The code database

A structured CPT/HCPCS/ICD dataset with plain-English text and price bands. This is a content moat and an SEO engine most competitors don't have.

Gap

No outcome yet

It explains and stores. It doesn't yet find the overcharge, reconcile against the EOB, or produce the appeal. That's the value users will pay for.

02

What it should, could, and can grow to do

Three honest columns: table stakes it's missing, differentiators within reach, and platform bets for later.

Should — but doesn't

Table stakes

  • Error & overcharge detectionFlag duplicate lines, unbundled codes, upcoding, quantity errors, out-of-network surprises. The #1 reason to open the app.
  • EOB ↔ bill reconciliationMatch the provider's bill to the insurer's Explanation of Benefits so "you're billed for what insurance already adjusted" is visible.
  • Deductible & out-of-pocket trackerRunning total across the plan year — the number every patient actually wants.
  • Appeal & dispute lettersTemplated letters per denial reason, prefilled from the bill.
  • Deadlines & remindersBill due dates and appeal windows (often 180 days) that cost money when missed.
  • Export / shareClean PDF summary for an FSA/HSA claim, a spouse, or an accountant.
Could — within reach

Differentiators

  • Real price benchmarkingWire the code DB to hospital price-transparency machine-readable files, CMS rates, and Fair Health so "typical price" becomes "here's the fair price near you."
  • Negotiation assistantScripts, phone-tree cheat sheets, and prewritten offers to settle. Later: success-fee negotiation on the user's behalf.
  • "Explain this bill" chatAn AI layer over the parsed bill + code DB that answers "why do I owe this?"
  • Denial-reason decoderTranslate CARC/RARC remittance codes into what to actually do next.
  • Financial-assistance finderMatch the provider to its charity-care policy and eligibility — many patients qualify and never ask.
Can extend to

Platform bets

  • Web app + extensionAlready started — capture portal bills where they actually live, and reach non-Apple users.
  • B2B2C white-labelSame engine inside an employer benefits hub, a health plan member app, or a TPA.
  • Data / API productLicense the code-explanation + price-benchmark API to other builders.
  • Medical-debt credit disputeHelp remove paid/erroneous medical collections from credit reports.
  • Adjacent billing worldsDental and veterinary bills — same parsing pain, far less regulation, real willingness to pay.

04

Bootstrapped go-to-market

How to grow with sweat, not spend. Ranked by return on a founder's hours.

With no ad budget, your unfair advantage is the code database and a founder who can ship. Turn both into a content machine and let high-intent search and desperate-at-2am forum readers find you. The full tactical playbook is a separate document — this is the strategy.

Programmatic SEO

Generate a page for every code and every "what is CPT 99213 / why am I billed for this" query straight from your database. Thousands of pages, each answering a real Google search, each funneling to the app. This is the single highest-leverage move you have.

$ · highest ROI

Community presence

Be genuinely helpful in r/personalfinance, r/MedicalBills, r/HealthInsurance, and patient Facebook groups. Answer bills, don't spam. Founder credibility compounds.

$ · time-heavy

Short-form video

"I fought a $4,200 ER bill down to $600 — here's exactly how." TikTok / Reels / Shorts. The medical-bill-win genre already performs; be the tool in the story.

$ · compounding

App Store Optimization

Title, subtitle, keyword field, screenshots that show an outcome, and a preview video. Free, high-intent traffic. Covered in depth in the growth playbook.

$ · essential

PR on the debt angle

Publish a small original data report from your price DB ("the 20 most-overpriced common procedures"). Journalists cover medical debt constantly and need data.

$ · spiky

Advocate partnerships

Patient advocates and nonprofits have the exact audience and no good tool. Offer them the pro tier free in exchange for referrals.

$ · relationship

Product Hunt + launch beats

A coordinated launch day for reach and backlinks, then a steady drumbeat of feature launches to stay visible.

$ · one-off

Deliberately excluded while bootstrapping: paid user acquisition beyond a small Apple Search Ads test. Don't buy traffic until a free user reliably becomes a paying one.

05

App → live: the chronological walkthrough

Eight phases in order. Don't start a phase until the one before it clears its gate.

0
Weeks 0–2 · Foundation

Stand up the business & the name

  • Resolve the name first. "BillGuard" was a well-known bill-monitoring app acquired by Prosper — clear the trademark or pick a clean brand (Guardia?) before you spend a dollar on it.
  • Form the entity (LLC), register the domain, set up email and a password manager for founder credentials.
  • Draft a privacy policy, terms, and a prominent medical/financial disclaimer ("not medical, legal, or financial advice").
  • Decide your HIPAA posture: a consumer app used by the patient is generally not a covered entity — but the moment you sell to providers/plans, HIPAA + BAAs apply.
Gate: legal name cleared, policies drafted, entity live.
1
Weeks 2–8 · Product

Cut v1 scope to one delightful outcome

  • Pick one hero outcome for v1 — realistically "scan a bill, understand every line, and flag the obvious problems." Ship that flawlessly instead of everything half-done.
  • Polish onboarding, empty states, and error copy. Make OCR failure graceful.
  • Build the paywall now: StoreKit 2, freemium with a subscription for the outcome features. Decide price.
  • Instrument a privacy-respecting analytics funnel: scan → understand → save → (later) act.
Gate: crash-free build, one outcome that makes a stranger say "oh, useful."
2
Weeks 6–9 · Compliance

Get store-ready

  • Enrol in the Apple Developer Program; set up App Store Connect.
  • Complete App Privacy labels honestly; health data has strict rules — never use it for ads or share it.
  • Add the medical disclaimer in-app and in the listing; review App Review guidelines for health & subscriptions.
  • Set the real CloudKit container and production environment (today's is a placeholder).
Gate: privacy labels done, disclaimers in place, production CloudKit live.
3
Weeks 8–12 · Beta

TestFlight with real bills

  • Internal TestFlight, then external — recruit 50–200 testers from the communities you'll later market to.
  • Watch them scan their own messy bills; OCR/parse breaks on real layouts, not clean samples.
  • Fix the top failure modes; confirm the funnel and the paywall convert.
Gate: testers succeed on their own bills; a few unprompted "I'd pay for this."
4
Weeks 10–14 · Pre-launch

Build the runway before you open the doors

  • Ship the landing page + waitlist and start the programmatic-SEO pages indexing (they take time to rank).
  • Produce ASO assets: icon, outcome-led screenshots, a 15–30s preview, keyword research.
  • Assemble a press kit and the original data report. Line up advocate partners and Product Hunt.
  • Seed 6–8 weeks of community answers and short videos so launch day isn't from zero.
Gate: waitlist growing, SEO pages indexing, launch assets done.
5
Launch week

Soft launch, then go loud

  • Soft-launch in one English market first to shake out crashes and store metrics.
  • Then coordinate: Product Hunt, the PR report, community posts, waitlist email, creator posts — same 48 hours.
  • Submit a featuring pitch to Apple (they love privacy-first, timely, well-designed apps).
Gate: stable at volume, reviews > 4.5, first organic installs from SEO/PR.
6
Weeks 1–8 post-launch

Stabilize & retain

  • Triage reviews and crashes daily; ship fast fixes. Early ratings set your ceiling.
  • Close the loop: prompt happy users for reviews right after a "win."
  • Double down on whichever acquisition channel actually moved installs; kill the rest.
Gate: D30 retention and free→paid conversion you can state with a straight face.
7
Month 3+ · Scale

Prove the loop, then decide on capital

  • Test a small paid-UA budget only once free→paid is reliable.
  • Ship the web app + extension to widen the market beyond Apple.
  • With traction in hand, open the fundraising conversation — from strength, not need. (See the investor dossier.)
Gate: repeatable acquisition + monetization → you're raising to accelerate, not to survive.
06

What to watch out for

The mistakes that sink health-money apps specifically — and how to route around each.

Legal / brand

The "BillGuard" name is already taken history

A prominent bill-monitoring app called BillGuard existed and was acquired by Prosper Marketplace. Shipping under that exact name invites a trademark problem and SEO confusion.

Route around it: run a trademark search now; strongly consider launching as Guardia (or another clean mark) before you build brand equity you'd have to throw away.

Compliance

Health-data rules & App Review

Apple treats health data strictly: it can't be used for advertising or sold, and mishandling it gets apps pulled. Health claims and subscription mechanics are common rejection reasons.

Route around it: honest privacy labels, no ad use of health data, clear disclaimers, and conservative claims. Never promise a specific dollar of savings.

Positioning

"Filing cabinet" won't sell a subscription

Organizing bills is nice-to-have; people churn off nice-to-haves. Willingness to pay lives in outcomes — caught errors, won appeals, money back.

Route around it: make v1's paywall gate an outcome, not storage. Measure "dollars flagged / saved," not "bills stored."

Liability

Accuracy of prices & error flags

Price benchmarks and "this looks like an overcharge" carry liability if presented as fact or advice.

Route around it: frame everything as "typical / worth questioning," cite sources, and keep the human in the loop. You surface questions; the user decides.

Platform risk

Apple-only caps your market

CloudKit-only means no Android and no true web parity — a real ceiling for a mass-market money-saving tool, and a question every investor asks.

Route around it: keep the parser/logic in the shared package (already the plan) so a web/Android path stays open; ship the web app early.

Trust & support

You're an indie holding sensitive data

One breach or a wave of "it charged me and did nothing" reviews can end a health-finance app. Support load is heavy in this category.

Route around it: lead with on-device privacy, set up real support from day one, and keep OCR expectations honest so reviews stay kind.

07

What to do Monday

If you only act on one section, act on this one.

  1. Run the trademark search on "BillGuard" and shortlist a clean name. Everything downstream depends on this.
  2. Pick v1's single outcome and write it on a sticky note. Cut every feature that isn't in service of it.
  3. Build one error-detection rule — duplicate line items is the easiest and most convincing. Ship the first "we found something."
  4. Stand up 25 programmatic code pages from your database as a proof of concept and submit a sitemap. Start the SEO clock now.
  5. Recruit 20 beta testers from one patient community and watch them scan real bills.
