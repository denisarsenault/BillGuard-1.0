Distribution & growth

Be the answer at 2 a.m. when the bill arrives.

People don't browse for a medical-bill app — they search in a panic, ask a forum, or watch someone win. This playbook saturates every one of those moments: high-intent search, earned trust, and loops that make each saved dollar recruit the next user.

Own the intent Earn the trust Loop the wins Then buy growth

The order matters. Bootstrapped growth is built from owned and earned channels first — the App Store listing, thousands of search-indexed pages, community credibility, and referral loops — because they compound and cost time, not money. Paid acquisition comes last, only once you know a user is worth more than they cost. Every section below is ranked for a founder spending hours, not dollars.

01

App Store Optimization

Your storefront is your biggest free channel. Most installs will decide here in seconds.

Rank and convert for the exact phrases a panicked patient types. Optimize the metadata Apple actually indexes, then make the screenshots sell an outcome, not a feature tour.

  • Title + subtitle carry the most weight — put your top keywords there, not just the brand.
  • Keyword field (100 chars): no spaces, no repeats, comma-separated, singular where possible.
  • Screenshots lead with the win; the first two are what most users see.
  • Preview video shows a real scan → "we found something" in 15 seconds.
  • Custom product pages tailored per audience (seniors, cancer patients) fed by different links.
  • Ratings prompt fired right after a user's first "win," never on cold launch.
  • Localize once one market works — cheap incremental installs.

02

Programmatic SEO — your compounding engine

The single biggest bootstrapped lever you have. Turn the code database into thousands of pages.

Every CPT, HCPCS and ICD code is something people Google in confusion. You already own a structured dataset that answers those queries. Generate a fast, static page for each — and for the questions around them — and you build an owned traffic asset that grows while you sleep and that competitors can't cheaply copy.

Page type 01

Code explainers

"What is CPT 99213? What it means, why you're billed, and the typical price." One page per code, thousands of pages, each targeting a real search.

Page type 02

Question & decoder pages

"How to dispute a hospital bill," "EOB vs bill," and a CARC/RARC denial-code decoder. Long-tail, high-intent, low-competition.

Page type 03

Provider & price pages

"Average cost of an MRI near you" fed by price-transparency data — the queries with buying intent.

Page type 04

The conversion hub

Every page ends the same way: "Scan your bill with BillGuard to check this automatically." Content → install.

Execution notes
  • Ship early — indexing and ranking take months; the sooner the pages exist, the sooner they earn.
  • Add schema markup (FAQ, medical) and tight internal linking between related codes.
  • Keep pages genuinely useful, not thin — Google punishes empty programmatic pages.
  • Submit a sitemap and monitor Search Console for what actually ranks, then expand there.
03

Content & video

The "fought my bill and won" genre already performs. Be the tool inside the story.

Short-form video

TikTok · Reels · Shorts

Founder- or creator-led: "I fought a $4,200 ER bill down to $600 — step by step." Show the app catching the error. Post consistently; one hit funds months of installs.

Long-form & UGC

YouTube & user stories

Deeper "how to read your medical bill" explainers, plus reposting users' own win screenshots (with permission). Real outcomes are the most persuasive ad you'll never pay for.

04

Community

Where confused patients already gather. Help first; the tool sells itself.

Show up as a genuinely useful expert, not a marketer. Answer real bill questions, share the "here's how I'd approach this" reasoning, and let people discover the app because you were helpful. Founder credibility in these spaces compounds into trust the App Store can't buy.

  • Reddit — r/personalfinance, r/MedicalBills, r/HealthInsurance, r/povertyfinance, disease-specific subs.
  • Facebook groups — patient and caregiver communities, chronic-illness support groups.
  • Disease-specific forums — cancer, dialysis, and rare-disease communities with relentless billing.
Rules of engagement
  • Give value 10× before ever mentioning the app.
  • Disclose you're the founder — honesty earns trust.
  • Never spam; one bad drop can get you banned from your best audience.
  • Run an AMA once you have credibility and wins to point to.
05

PR & earned media

Journalists cover medical debt constantly and are starved for fresh data. Give them some.

Angle 01

Original data report

Mine your price DB: "The 20 most-overpriced common procedures," "the widest price gaps by city." Reporters cite data; a citation is a backlink and a wave of installs.

Angle 02

Reporter sourcing

Answer journalist queries (HARO/Qwoted and similar) as an expert on billing errors. Steady, free earned media.

Angle 03

Local & timely

Pitch "how to fight your medical bill" around tax season and open enrollment — evergreen segments local news always needs.

06

Creators & partnerships

Borrow trusted audiences instead of building from zero.

Creators

Personal-finance & patient advocates

Micro-influencers in personal finance and chronic-illness advocacy convert far above their follower count because their audience is exactly your user.

Nonprofits

Advocacy & charity-care orgs

Groups that help people with medical debt and charity care have your audience and no good tool. Offer the pro tier free for referrals.

Adjacent products

FSA/HSA & benefits

Co-marketing with FSA/HSA providers and benefits platforms — complementary, non-competing, same user.

07

Paid acquisition — last, not first

Only buy growth once a free user reliably becomes a paying one. Then start where intent is highest.

Start here

Apple Search Ads

Highest-intent inventory there is — someone searching "medical bill help" in the App Store is ready to install. Start with brand defense + a tight set of exact keywords, measure cost-per-paying-user, and scale only what pays back.

Then, carefully

Meta & TikTok

Once you know LTV, use the "fought my bill and won" creative to acquire at scale. Guardrail: keep payback under your target window; pause anything that doesn't.

Rule: never scale a channel whose cost-per-paying-user exceeds what that user is worth. Paid is an amplifier for a working loop, never a substitute for one.

08

Referral & virality loops

Make each saved dollar recruit the next user.

Loop 01

Shareable win

After a caught error, offer a clean "I saved $312 with BillGuard" card to share. Social proof and acquisition in one tap.

Loop 02

Family & caregiver invites

Multi-patient already exists — let users invite a spouse or an adult child managing a parent's bills. Households spread naturally.

Loop 03

Give-get referral

Give a free month to both sides. Simple, measurable, and cheap versus paid installs.

09

Lifecycle & reviews

Getting the install is half the job; keeping and reactivating is the other half.

Keep them
  • Onboarding to first "win" as fast as possible.
  • Deadline push for appeal windows — genuinely useful, drives re-open.
  • New-bill nudge — "got a new bill? scan it."
  • Win-back for lapsed users when a new feature ships.
Grow the rating
  • Ask after a win, never on a cold launch.
  • Route unhappy users to support before the review box.
  • Reply to reviews — Apple lets you, and it converts skeptics.
  • High ratings feed ASO — reviews are a growth channel, not an afterthought.
10

Get featured by Apple

An App Store feature is the biggest free install spike available. Earn it deliberately.

Why you're a fit

Apple loves privacy-first, beautifully designed apps that solve a real human problem — BillGuard's on-device story and clean SwiftUI design are exactly the pitch editors respond to.

How to ask

Nominate your app through App Store Connect ahead of launch, tie it to a timely moment (tax season, open enrollment), lead with the privacy angle, and send editors a crisp story with great assets.

12

The first 90 days

A concrete launch cadence. Owned and earned first; paid only once the loop proves out.

Month 1 SEED

  • Ship 50+ SEO pages and submit the sitemap — start the clock.
  • Finalize ASO: title, subtitle, keywords, outcome screenshots, preview.
  • Join the communities and start answering — no pitching yet.
  • Draft the data report and build the press list.
  • Set up analytics, RevenueCat, and the review-prompt-after-win.

Month 2 LAUNCH

  • Launch day: Product Hunt + PR report + community posts + waitlist email, same 48h.
  • Submit the Apple featuring nomination.
  • Publish 3–5 short videos; seed the first "win" stories.
  • Line up 2–3 creators / advocate partners.
  • Triage reviews & crashes daily; ship fast fixes.

Month 3 AMPLIFY

  • Turn on referral (shareable win + give-get).
  • Small Apple Search Ads test on exact keywords — measure cost per paying user.
  • Double down on the one channel that actually moved installs; cut the rest.
  • Expand SEO into the pages Search Console shows ranking.
  • Ship a feature as a fresh launch beat and PR hook.
